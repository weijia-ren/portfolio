## What this poster covers

- Estimands, populations, parameters, and point estimates
- Sampling variability, standard deviation, and standard error
- Confidence intervals and hypothesis tests
- Type I/II errors, p-values, large-N interpretation, and effect sizes
- Power, minimum detectable effect, one-sided vs. two-sided tests, and multiplicity
- Basic test selection, t vs. z tests, skewed data, mean vs. median
- Missing data, outliers, collinearity, nonparametric methods, and frequentist vs. Bayesian framing
- Interview traps, a method-selection flow, a 30-second answer, and a 10-second recall board

## Why it matters for Quant UXR

In product research, statistics is most useful when it clarifies decisions under uncertainty. A strong analysis starts with the estimand, connects measurement to the user or product question, and explains uncertainty in language stakeholders can act on.

## 30-second interview framing

Statistics helps us estimate unknown quantities and make decisions under uncertainty. I start by defining the estimand: what population quantity or product effect we actually care about. Then I choose a design and model that match the data-generating process, quantify uncertainty with intervals or posterior distributions, and separate statistical significance from practical impact. In product settings, the goal is not just to get a small p-value; it is to produce valid, decision-relevant evidence that explains what changed, for whom, and whether the change is meaningful enough to act on.

## Common traps this poster helps prevent

- Treating a metric as the estimand without defining the underlying decision
- Confusing standard deviation with standard error
- Interpreting confidence intervals as probability statements about a fixed parameter
- Equating statistical significance with practical importance
- Ignoring multiplicity when many metrics or segments are tested
- Treating missing data and outliers as purely technical cleanup instead of validity risks
